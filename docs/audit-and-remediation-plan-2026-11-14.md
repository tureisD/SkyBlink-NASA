## Assessment

SkyBlink has a substantial working implementation: FastAPI discovery and FITS processing, five saved real-data target datasets, a React canvas viewer, spectra, reference overlays, flags, and exports. The existing engineering checks pass. However, “complete,” “any coordinates,” “scientifically validated,” and “no synthetic data anywhere” are stronger claims than the evidence supports.

The highest priorities are removing synthetic products from the shipped application, enabling actual temporal comparison at arbitrary positions, correcting the frontend sky projection, and validating comparisons at compatible wavelengths. The five saved target datasets are useful, but passing their current acceptance card is not independent confirmation of every scientific interpretation.

## Findings

### F1 — Synthetic data remains in the repository and shipping paths — P0

Evidence:

- `golden_cache/fallback_*`: five directories, 30 files each. Their generator explicitly creates synthetic stars, backgrounds, motion, and flags.
- `frontend/public/mock`: 34 files, copied into `frontend/dist/mock` by the verified build.
- `Dockerfile` copies the whole golden cache and built frontend; `.dockerignore` excludes neither data location.
- `/api/v1/assets/fallback_wise_0855/manifest.json` and `/api/v1/assets/fallback_wise_0855/2025_E1_ALL.webp` returned 200 in the real application router.
- `frontend/src/data/eps_indi_templates.json` contains generated Planck curves. `ForwardComparator.tsx` exposes them in the ordinary application, clearly labeled illustrative. They are not measured T1/T6 spectra.
- Backend tests construct artificial arrays/FITS inputs. These are test inputs, not NASA observations, but they also prevent a literal claim of no synthetic data anywhere.

The normal sky/spectrum hooks do not automatically fall back to fixtures, and the golden manifest loader rejects a true simulation marker. Those protections are useful but do not establish absence of synthetic assets. The product's current policy is weaker than the requested condition.

### F2 — Arbitrary positions default to one epoch — P0

`backend/core/config.py:88` sets `live_sky_max_epochs = 1`; sky and spectrum routes use it. `build_manifest()` discovers epochs and retains only the latest N. A successful default live response therefore cannot blink between dates or produce a temporal difference. This is a functional gap even if all HTTP requests succeed.

Live mode also disables SIA2 (`fallback=not live_mode`). The two fresh probes failed cleanly because of upstream/proxy errors; neither establishes missing sky coverage. Earlier project timings are historical evidence, not today's result.

### F3 — Frontend WCS is unsuitable for unrestricted coordinates — P0

`frontend/src/lib/wcs.ts:36` uses a linear RA/cos(dec) approximation instead of the backend's TAN projection. Reproducing that formula at a corner of the full grid and comparing it with Astropy gives approximately 1.70 arcsec error at declination 66.75° and 8.35 arcsec at 85°. At a center declination of 89.9°, it returns 90.00207° for that corner, an invalid declination; the true TAN value is about 89.89791°.

This affects cursor coordinates and any selection/overlay using these helpers. It does not show that the backend FITS reprojection itself is wrong. Even the R Draconis latitude exceeds the file's claimed “well under 0.1 pixel” approximation accuracy near the grid edge.

### F4 — Cross-epoch detector frames need matched spectral sampling — P0 science gate

`_select_visits()` selects records by temporal spacing, not wavelength compatibility. The saved WISE 0855 B6 mean wavelengths are 4.9435, 4.6255, and 4.8959 µm; B1 means are 1.0106, 0.7731, and 0.9629 µm. Thus the same detector key does not imply the same effective spectral bandpass.

Inference: differing spectral samples can leave residuals around static objects with different spectral shapes, even after shared percentile normalization. Direct differences and brightness changes cannot automatically be interpreted as variability. The six-month buckets also mix dates over a long interval; selected timestamps differ across bands. This needs measured controls, especially for fast movers and spectral features.

### F5 — Coadd weighting ignores the declared photometry exclusion mask — P1, before cache rebuild

`backend/core/flags.py` describes `PHOTOMETRY_EXCLUDE_MASK` as applying to photometry and coadds. `spherex_pipeline.py:721` weights finite images with positive variance but does not exclude DICHROIC or other warning bits. Parsing retains warning pixels for display. Consequently those values can contribute to coadds and raw difference products. Spectral extraction does apply the conservative mask.

Resolve display visibility separately from measurement eligibility: keeping an artifact visible does not require accepting its flux into a science coadd. HALO's deliberate display exception must remain separate from measurement masks.

### F6 — Current acceptance tests overstate scientific validation — P1

The five-target card compares RMS and motion signals in rendered 8-bit WebP values. WISE 0855 and Epsilon Indi tests compare band RMS ratios even though the difference renderer assigns band-specific display limits. These ratios depend on stretches and are not physical flux ratios or calibrated significance estimates. Some tests establish configured facts, such as a known pair being smaller than a pixel, rather than measuring them.

`test_astrometry.py` checks self-consistency of stored coordinates, propagation, and output WCS. I did not locate an independent image-centroid versus at-least-three-Gaia-star residual gate in the reviewed validation code. Gaia pins alone do not supply that test.

The cache's accepted/total spectral channel counts were reproduced:

| Target | Used / total channels | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| WISE 0855 | 158 / 164 | Existing card explicitly warns of aperture contamination. |
| Barnard's Star | 2 / 215 | Saturation severely limits the spectrum. |
| Epsilon Indi B | 228 / 246 | Many usable channels; blending remains. |
| RW Aurigae | 102 / 216 | Substantial exclusions. |
| R Draconis | 0 / 240 | No accepted photometry, despite the target card passing. |

These are eligibility counts, not independent proof of uncontaminated source flux. A high cache-quality label should not be presented as high measurement quality.

### F7 — Provenance and data semantics need tightening — P1

- Sky and spectrum routers replace the stored generation timestamp with the request time (`sky.py:331`, `spectrum.py:156`). An old observation product therefore appears freshly generated.
- Image manifests retain source URIs in band statistics, but saved spectrum channels lack the exposure identifier/MJD needed to trace individual measurements independently.
- Telemetry is generated from normalized coadds (`spherex_pipeline.py:1407`) while the UI calls it physical brightness. It needs an explicit normalization disclosure; it must not be treated as raw calibrated flux.
- `2024_E1` represents actual April 2025 data for two targets, due to the November–April bucketing scheme. This is not evidence of fabricated prelaunch observations, but the label is misleading.
- Release metadata for spectra is derived from discovered epoch records rather than explicitly from the successfully extracted subset. Failed downloads can make the claimed release mixture broader than the contributing data.

### F8 — Discovery completeness and deadlines are not guaranteed — P1

TAP uses `SELECT TOP 5000` ordered oldest first. A dense field exceeding this cap can omit newer observations, despite the later code selecting the “latest” epoch. No paging/completeness mechanism is evident in this query path.

Deadlines are checked between stages, rather than propagated as a remaining budget through every operation. Download workers wait for their futures; processing after the sky path's last deadline check can overrun the nominal deadline. Increasing the epoch budget without addressing this would compound latency and worker occupancy. The all-failed/partially-failed SIA fallback classification also deserves explicit outage-versus-empty tests.

### F9 — Background subtraction is simpler than the stated contract — P1/P2 by measured impact

`parse_cutout()` subtracts ZODI and a single masked median, not a residual 2D background. When there are ten or fewer valid background pixels it subtracts zero, yet provenance still claims background subtraction. Gradients and crowded fields need a measured quality gate. Spectral uncertainties sum per-pixel variance but omit uncertainty in the local background estimate; keep their approximate status explicit.

#### 4.1 Assessment: the foundation is useful, but not sufficient yet

The current implementation provides real source products, fixed saved datasets,
asset hashes, source code, regression coverage and reproducible audit scripts.
These are a strong starting point. They do **not** yet establish that a new
reviewer can reconstruct every reported measurement, restore the exact analysis,
or understand the limits without help from the original authors.

Separate four promises:

| Promise | Current foundation | Remaining gap |
|---|---|---|
| Recover the same saved artifact | Approved hashes for 288 saved real files | Hashes establish identity, not complete upstream lineage or scientific validity |
| Recompute a scientific quantity | Processing code and real-input audit scripts | Per-channel/exposure lineage, exact configuration/environment and reproducible input retention are incomplete |
| Restore another person's analysis | URL state, tables and PNG/Markdown export | Selected difference pair, display/camera settings and data revision are not fully preserved; spectrum and sky selection may differ |
| Independently understand and assess the result | Flags, provenance panels and audit reports | Units/telemetry wording, historical claims and quality scope can mislead; locked control test still fails |

Examples already confirmed in the audit: routers replace `generated_at` with
request time; some exact raw audit inputs exist only in ignored local caches;
the saved products were not regenerated by Phase 3; native spectra do not
necessarily describe the image pair on screen; and a valid archive origin does
not certify a variability claim. Do not declare these issues fixed by writing
more documentation or by adding a “reproducible” badge.

#### 4.2 Define a replayable scientific record

Create a versioned internal analysis record and a documented, safe subset for
the UI/export. This is a design requirement; proposed fields need the existing
API review/version process before becoming public contract fields. It does not
require a database, distributed workflow engine or large FITS files in Git.

Each record must preserve:

1. **Identity and time:** a stable result identifier/fingerprint; input retrieval
   time, observation times/ranges and product creation time as distinct concepts;
   API, cache-schema, dataset revision and processing-method versions. Keep
   response/access time separate. Define which fields contribute to identity;
   request timestamps must not change a scientific product fingerprint.
2. **Inputs:** exact exposure/catalog/calibration identifiers and URLs, release
   and processing version, cutout parameters, content hashes and byte sizes.
   Record catalog query/reference epoch and any cached catalog response needed
   for the result. Preserve unknown historic fields honestly until rebuilt.
3. **Coordinates:** supplied position, canonical query position, reference
   astrometric solution, propagation assumptions, and actual per-exposure
   extraction position. Store actual WCS/footprint context and requested crop.
4. **Selection:** discovered candidate set within the actual search bounds,
   completeness status, considered/selected/rejected inputs and reason codes,
   selected observations/bands, spectral matching criteria and time spans.
   Record failed downloads and insufficient support; rerunning a query for
   “latest” observations is not a replay of a previous selection.
5. **Method:** code identity, relevant configuration, mask bit definitions,
   background method/support, native aperture/annulus, calibration identity,
   resampling/variance policy, combination weights and display-normalization
   parameters. Distinguish raw measurement processing from visualization.
6. **Outputs and limits:** asset hashes, channel-level links to their inputs,
   units, flags, eligibility/rejection reasons, approximate uncertainty method,
   and validation status with its scope. Record which spectrum inputs overlap
   the displayed image inputs instead of implying they are identical.

An exposure index referenced by many channels can avoid duplicated metadata.
Choose a compact readable schema and document it. The exported subset should
contain what is needed to verify the result without including local absolute
paths, credentials or unrelated environment settings.

#### 4.3 Build a reproducible execution path

Deliver an explicitly named replay command/workflow that consumes a pinned
analysis record and verified inputs. Its name and arguments are implementation
decisions, not commands that already exist. It must:

- Resolve exact input bytes by hash from a documented cache or download them
  from recorded URLs; verify identity before processing. If an upstream file
  changed, return an explicit mismatch, not a silently updated result.
- Support offline replay for the curated real example after its inputs have
  been acquired. Keep a manifest of required files and a supported acquisition
  command; do not rely on an author's ignored working cache being present.
- Define where the curated raw-input bundle is retained outside Git and how
  a reviewer obtains it. Record size and retention/access conditions. If those
  inputs cannot be made available, describe the result as artifact-verifiable
  or partially reproducible rather than claiming complete recomputation.
- Capture the environment actually used: Python/Node versions where relevant,
  resolved dependencies, platform and relevant library/encoder versions. Use
  a locked environment or equivalent frozen dependency record for the supported
  replay path. A container is useful but does not replace input or method identity.
- Record a commit plus patch/content digest when the tree is dirty; a commit
  identifier alone must not pretend to describe uncommitted scientific changes.
- Write outputs to a new location, never over the approved cache. Compare
  selected inputs, coordinates, masks, null/eligibility states and scientific
  values before considering display images.
- Distinguish **artifact identity** (exact hashes for retained files),
  **numerical reproducibility** (declared tolerances in a named environment),
  and **scientific validation** (independent controls/calibration). Set numerical
  tolerances before inspecting replay differences, justify them by the method,
  and do not use loose tolerances to hide changed selection or masks. Encoded
  images need not be byte-identical across platforms to reproduce numeric
  science; report that distinction explicitly.

Preserve one usable curated comparison and one unusable/null measurement as
replay cases, chosen after the scientific repair. If no suitable passing
comparison exists, keep that blocker visible rather than choosing a visually
dramatic but scientifically incompatible pair. Maintain historical failed
records so the improvement can be inspected. Arbitrary live fields remain
dependent on coverage and upstream availability; bounded local retention is
not a promise of permanent replay for every possible request. Define expiry
and missing-result behavior for those records.

#### 4.4 Make the result explainable before styling it

For the two replay cases, write a short evidence walkthrough using actual
values: question → selected inputs → why they can/cannot be compared → measured
quantity → uncertainty and exclusions → supported conclusion → unresolved
alternatives. Include a machine-readable record, a human-readable summary and
the method/source links. A reviewer should not need to read every module to
explain why a flux is null or a comparison is limited.

Create a single reviewed dictionary of terms and reason codes for the backend,
UI, exports and documentation. At minimum cover data origin, coverage,
comparison eligibility, measurement eligibility, background support,
approximate error, normalized intensity and validation scope. Prefer specific
reasons over a generic “low confidence.” Reference guides and catalog motion
are context, not measured detections. Keep unknown, failed, insufficient and
not evaluated distinct.

Provide the UI team a capability map with real examples of every supported
state. The UI must not invent certainty or infer meaning from an enum's color,
cache location or descriptive prose. Phase 5 improves how this information is
presented; it must not have to reconstruct the scientific truth from scattered
metadata. Link the resulting walkthrough and dictionary into the
[documentation writing plan](plans/documentation-writing-plan.md).

| Package | Deliverable | Primary responsibility | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| R0 | Resolve coordinate/method decisions and independent validation blockers | Science + backend + Orchestrator | Approved reference solution and method; failed evidence retained; no unsupported promotion |
| R1 | Versioned analysis/lineage record and timestamp policy | Backend + science | Each displayed/extracted product maps to exact inputs and method; public changes reviewed |

| R3 | Staged rebuild and migration/rollback comparison | Science + backend | New products satisfy physical, structural and provenance gates before replacement |
| R4 | Evidence walkthroughs, vocabulary and UI capability handoff | Science + documentation + frontend | Reviewer can explain usable and unusable results and locate their evidence |

Promotion requires all of the following, in addition to the scientific gates:

- Someone other than the implementer follows the documented workflow in a
  clean environment and reproduces the curated numerical results within the
  predeclared tolerances. Record environment, commands, outcome and deviations.
  If an independent reviewer is unavailable, mark this gate unverified.
- Repeated requests preserve creation time, input/output hashes and result
  identity. Only fields explicitly defined as response-time metadata change.
- No displayed measurement is detached from its contributing exposure(s),
  extraction position, method and eligibility. A saved file hash alone is not
  accepted as the full lineage chain.
- Missing/changed upstream inputs produce an actionable failure or an explicitly
  limited artifact-only verification; they cannot silently substitute data.
- The reviewer can locate dates, sampled wavelengths, background/mask policy,
  units, approximate errors and limitations in the walkthrough without reading
  source code. They can correctly explain why the unusable case is unavailable.
- Scientific arrays and interpretation remain independent of display settings.
  Phase 5 then verifies faithful restoration of the documented view state and
  consistency between workspace, exported report and this analysis record.

**Handoff to the UI phase:** Supply a reviewed inventory of the metadata the UI can actually use: observation ranges, selected pair/bands, data revision, source lineage, comparison eligibility, photometry availability, background support, telemetry meaning and validation scope. Some are currently internal or missing. Propose any public additions through the API decision/version process; do not make the frontend infer scientific status from filenames, color, a cache hit or free-text warning strings. Document unknown values explicitly. Freeze the measurement meaning before rewriting result copy.

#### 5.1 Positioning: what will actually distinguish this project

**Chosen layout revision, 2026-11-15:** the user explicitly prefers the blink
image in the middle with sidebars. Implement that composition with purposeful

two-column shell while retaining its meaning and accessibility requirements:

  comparison limitation, actual dates and playback/band controls close to the
  image; the initial canvas must start within a normal desktop viewport.
- The left rail owns field selection/navigation and source context. The right
  rail owns interpretation and progressively disclosed inspection tools. Avoid
  repeating the same warning or controls in every panel.
- Let users collapse either rail or focus on the viewer. These operations must
  preserve the selected source, pair, band, camera and persistent primary canvas.
  Collapsed rails must have visible, keyboard-operable restore controls.
- Essential source/comparison warnings remain outside collapsible specialist
  details. A single epoch cannot gain a Play action through a layout change.
- At tablet and phone sizes, offer readable stacked/panel access, not three
  compressed columns. Preserve 320px reflow and touch/keyboard access.
- Verify actual image/column geometry at 1440px and 1920px, no primary action
  loss at 1024/390/320px, canvas identity through rail/focus/playback changes,
  explicit A/B and difference dates, recovery and modal focus. Capture real-data
  screenshots with source hashes. Browser checks still do not close the human
  comprehension or assistive-technology acceptance gates.

The remaining non-release work is prioritized separately in
[current limitations and next steps](current-limitations-and-next-steps.md).
These proposals do not authorize silent calibration changes or replacement of
the historical saved datasets.

The user's emphasis on UI quality, clear results and easy navigation is
directionally right. The stronger proposition is **making a defensible
comparison understandable and reproducible**. A beautiful interface that
confuses a wavelength change with time variability is weak; mathematically
careful software that users cannot interpret is also incomplete. Compete on
the combination: useful task completion, substantive NASA data use, scientific
honesty and a clear demonstration of the team's decisions.

Do not assume competitors' methods or claim that presentation alone decides
the winner. The official [2025 judging guide](https://spaceapps-v2-staging.s3.amazonaws.com/media/documents/Final_Space_Apps_2025_Judging_Guidelines_for_web.pdf)
describes holistic assessment informed by impact, creativity, validity,
relevance and presentation; validity includes usability/accessibility. It does
not establish the four equal weights quoted in the historical roadmap. This
is **2025 context only**. The [2026 challenge](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/planet-x-and-spherex/),
[current judging guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/judging-awards-guide/)
and [submission guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/)
returned HTTP 502 during this review. Reverify current rules at Phase 6; neither
the roadmap nor a local-event page substitutes for the global guide.

The UI must help someone answer these questions in order:

1. What object or field am I examining?
2. Which observations, dates and spectral samples am I comparing?
3. Is this comparison usable, limited, unavailable or still unvalidated?
4. What difference can I see or measure, and what else could explain it?
5. How can another person reproduce and inspect this evidence?

The desired conclusion may be **“inconclusive”** or **“no usable measurement.”**
Making those outcomes clear is a successful user experience, not a failure to
produce an exciting result.

#### 5.3 Target information architecture

Use one workspace with progressively revealed detail, rather than two
independent novice/expert applications. A guided view and an expanded analysis
view must share the same field, observations, quality facts and export state.

**Always visible:** target/search; source/data revision; actual selected date
range; active wavelength context; one concise comparison-status sentence;
play/pause/step; access to Evidence and Help. Keep attribution compact and
reachable. Do not put essential warnings only in tooltips or collapsed panels.

**Primary tasks:**

| Task | Main content | Secondary controls |
|---|---|---|
| Observe | One dated image, orientation/scale, target marker, readable pixel-inspection state | Display adjustment, band, reference overlay |
| Compare | Explicit A/B selection; synchronized blink or side-by-side; difference when eligible | Shared display range, artifact overlay, registration/coverage details |
| Spectrum | Actual finite/usable samples, approximate errors, selected observation scope | Raw table, flags, reference wavelength guides, CSV |
| Evidence | What is observed, what is uncertain, source inputs, methods and export | Full provenance, validation report, technical parameters |

This is a proposed task model, not a mandatory four-tab implementation. Test
whether tabs, a segmented control or an inspector better supports switching
without losing context. Avoid forcing users to close a large modal just to
compare a spectrum with its image. A docked/resizable inspector on desktop and
a full-height sheet on mobile are candidates for testing.

Move colormap/stretch/bias, full flag bit lists, LVF geometry, trial motion,
parallax and audio into named advanced groups. Keep them accessible in one or
two deliberate actions. Explain “trial/assumed motion” before exposing controls;
these must not look like measured velocity or distance. Preserve useful
keyboard shortcuts and a visible “Reset view” operation.

Proposed desktop structure:

```text
[SkyBlink] [Target or coordinates / Search] [Data + comparison status] [Help]
[Optional target list] [Image or synchronized A/B viewport] [Evidence inspector]
                      [Dates A/B | Step | Play | Band | Mode]
                      [Concise limitation / next useful action]
```

Proposed phone structure:

```text
[Target / Search] [Quality summary]
[Image with compact date and band labels]
[Previous | Play/Pause | Next] [A / B date selection]
[Observe | Compare | Spectrum | Evidence]
[Contextual sheet for band, quality or advanced controls]
```

No essential action may require discovering a horizontally scrolling ribbon.
Reserve canvas space; do not replace tiny HUD text with large overlays that
obscure the source. Show pin/readout details outside the pixels when possible.

#### 5.4 User journeys to design and test

**First-time visitor:** start paused with a brief explanation and two clear
actions: open a reviewed real example or enter coordinates. Explain the example's
purpose (“learn to compare observations” or “see why saturation prevents a
measurement”), not a promised discovery. Display actual dates and comparison
status before Play. After comparison, point to flags/evidence and state what
cannot yet be concluded. Make the tour dismissible and replayable; do not
block access with a forced slideshow.

**Experienced user:** paste coordinates, confirm parsed ICRS degrees and FOV,
query, inspect available observations/bands, choose a supported comparison,
inspect quantitative results and source lineage, export. Keep advanced state
sticky only within appropriate context; a new field must not inherit a stale
selected source, epoch pair or spectrum. Show exact settings in details rather
than forcing a beginner to parse them.

**Unusable result:** explain whether the problem is no coverage, no compatible
sampled pair, insufficient clean background, saturation, unavailable reference
data or upstream failure. Offer the next meaningful action. “Try a smaller
field” applies to a comparison/coverage limitation, not every server error.
“Choose a saved example” must be a real button, not just text.

**Judge's short visit:** reach a reviewed comparison, identify the data and
dates, understand one result and one limitation, and open a reproducible report
without reading the methods chapter. Do not choose the example until Phase 4
establishes what it supports. A clearly explained unusable measurement can
demonstrate honesty, but the main demonstration must still solve the core
temporal-inspection task with real suitable data.

#### 5.5 Establish an explicit evidence and quality model

Maintain four independent dimensions, each with a readable label and reason:

| Dimension | Example states | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Saved real observations; live archive observations; source unavailable | Scientific validity or complete provenance by itself |
| Comparison eligibility | Compatible selection; limited coverage; one observation; incompatible sampled pair; legacy/unvalidated | Astrophysical change or identical PSFs |
| Measurement availability | Usable approximate flux; partial/excluded measurement; no usable background; saturated/null | Calibrated total flux or independent detection probability |
| Validation scope | Checked on named sample; failed control gate; insufficient checks; not evaluated | Universal validation of all fields/releases |

Do not replace these dimensions with a single green “trusted” score. Status
must apply to the selected pixel/aperture/band/epoch/pair, not silently to the
entire project. State whether artifact fraction is frame-wide while status
is aperture-based. Expose specific reasons and relevant counts; do not label
an absent result “clean.” A catalog/cache “high confidence” tier must not become
a scientific-quality badge.

Design an Evidence summary with four short blocks:

- **Compared:** actual dates/ranges, selected band samples and coverage.
- **Observed:** literal visual or numerical fact, with units when measured.
- **Limits:** artifacts, wavelength/PSF mismatch, background, blending,
  approximate uncertainty or unvalidated historical processing.
- **Next step:** inspect the flagged region, choose another eligible pair,
  check provenance, export for review or obtain independent follow-up.

Initial summaries should be deterministic, evidence-based templates reviewed
by science, not generated discovery narratives. A user annotation must be
distinguished from a measured fact. Unknown metadata is shown as unknown; do
not parse an unsupported scientific conclusion from a story headline.

Define a reviewed backend-to-frontend capability map before implementation.
Today the public schema does not necessarily expose all `comparison_selection`,
background diagnostics or per-channel lineage. Specify field semantics, scope,
missing-value behavior, backward compatibility and evidence source before
adding fields. Old revision-3 payloads must default to **not evaluated/legacy**,
not pass. API changes require a written decision and versioning; no current
contract change is authorized merely by this planning document.

#### 5.6 Repair the comparison and spectrum experiences

**Temporal comparison:** show A and B as actual date ranges, with elapsed time
and selected composition. Keep stable internal epoch IDs in details; do not
teach users that `2024_E1` is an observation date. Explain whether the timeline
is ordinal or time-proportional; equal spacing must not imply equal gaps.
Make one-epoch data useful for observation but disable temporal conclusions.

Offer manual stepping and side-by-side as primary accessible alternatives to
blinking. Keep shared pan/zoom and visual transfer settings across A/B;
differences must name subtraction order and use a signed legend with a clear
zero. Show footprint gaps as unavailable, not physically dark pixels. Do not
silently compare different detector combinations or enable Difference solely
because a legacy asset exists. If exploratory legacy differences remain
accessible, mark them visibly unvalidated and exclude discovery language.

**Wavelength context:** label detector ranges separately from actual sampled
wavelengths. “ALL” means available contributing bands, not necessarily all six
or all mission channels. “Thermal” is a product label for the B5/B6 composite,
not measured temperature; show actual contributing bands. Use small coverage
summaries and expandable detail instead of crowding every wavelength onto the
canvas.

**Spectrum:** distinguish number of recorded samples, finite fluxes and usable
measurements. Default to data points and approximate error bars; break lines
across gaps/ineligible samples and make connecting lines optional. Include
negative measured values and full finite error extents. Do not hide low flux
by forcing a zero minimum. State that wavelength coverage is sparse and the
aperture measurement can blend sources. Use a well-defined scale when no valid
points exist, but show an unavailable explanation instead of an empty molecular
chart. Wavelength/molecule reference guides must be labeled “reference
positions—not detections,” and be off by default.

For partial/null results show reasons, clean fraction and measurement
eligibility separately. Enable keyboard selection of plotted points and an
equivalent sortable/readable table. Retain CSV for unusable channels as metadata
if useful, with a label that does not suggest measured flux. Align exported
statistics with `is_used_in_photometry` and the approved science policy.

**Telemetry/reference overlays:** name the plane being sampled and whether it
matches the displayed band. Phase 4 must settle normalized-versus-raw semantics;
show units and normalization accordingly. DSS/WISE references have different
dates, passbands and resolutions; do not use a curtain alone as a quantitative
variability test. Gaia pins and exaggerated direction ticks are catalog
annotations, not SPHEREx detections or measured epoch displacement.

#### 5.7 Persistent loading, failure and recovery states

Build an explicit state table and test it before polishing transitions:

| State | What stays available | Message/action |
|---|---|---|
| Idle/first visit | Search, saved examples, Help | Choose a field; explain what happens next |
| Query in progress | Search, Cancel, selected query, saved examples | Honest elapsed time and overall budget; no invented percentage |
| Partial assets available | Loaded image with specific pending/failed layer labels | Retry failed layer; do not represent missing spectrum/reference as absent sky flux |
| One epoch | Image, date, flags, spectrum if available | No temporal comparison available; disable Play/Difference with explanation |
| No comparable sampled pair | Search/FOV/observation details | Bounded search found no compatible pair; not proof the archive contains none |
| No observations | Search and coordinate editing | Explain coverage result and exact query |
| Upstream outage/timeout | Retry, Cancel/new query, saved examples | Archive unavailable or budget exceeded; do not claim no data |
| Insufficient measurement support | Image/evidence and metadata table | Explain null/excluded photometry without inventing values |
| Stale/cancelled request | New query controls | Old completion cannot replace the new field; stale visible view explicitly labeled |
| Export failure | Current valid view and retry | Explain failed report generation without losing the analysis |

Current endpoints do not stream detailed processing progress. Use elapsed
time/indeterminate status; only show “downloading” or “calibrating” when actual
backend events support that statement. Browser cancellation may only stop
waiting; document whether backend shared work continues. Do not launch duplicate
expensive builds on repeated clicks. If background jobs are later introduced,
that is a separate backend/API decision with its own resource/cancellation
model, not a fake progress animation.

#### 5.8 Visual design and responsive behavior

Keep the restrained dark astronomical workspace and large image area, but
replace pervasive microtype with a clear hierarchy. Prototype 14–16px primary
reading text, 12–14px secondary metadata and readable numeric monospace;
reserve very small type for nonessential detail. Use consistent spacing,
section rhythm, concise sentence-case labels and a limited semantic palette.
Quality must use text/icons in addition to color. Distinguish selection,
warning and source origin rather than using one accent for all three.

Reduce default Gaia/reticle clutter; keep scale/orientation available. Place
target identity, selected observations and the important limitation ahead of
grid dimensions, raw proper-motion components and display transfer controls.
Explain unfamiliar terms locally: “Compare dates,” “Pixel warnings,” “Infrared
samples,” and “Advanced display” can introduce technical vocabulary without
changing its scientific meaning.

At phone widths, use accessible sheets for band/date selection and analysis;
keep a compact playback row visible. Ensure sheets can be dismissed without
losing state, safe-area insets are respected, and the software keyboard does
not hide validation/actions. Support ordinary vertical page scrolling; require
an intentional gesture/mode for canvas panning where needed. Test portrait,
landscape and browser zoom. A larger viewport is not permission to duplicate
every control in both rails.

The existing AGENTS §5.7 design contract prescribes 10px headings and specific
rail/deck layouts. This phase proposes changing those presentation requirements
where usability evidence supports it. Record the Orchestrator's updated design
decision and policy revision before implementation; preserve science semantics
and canvas performance guardrails. The plan itself does not silently supersede
the current contract.

##### 5.8.1 A deliberate visual identity, not a generic dashboard

Treat the user's “less AI-slop/vibe-coded” request as a design-quality brief,
not a claim about how any particular visual element was authored. The goal is
a recognizable, carefully composed scientific tool. Removing gradients alone
will not achieve that; hierarchy, wording, proportion and task flow matter more.
The current UI already avoids many generic dashboard tropes, but its dense
mission-console styling, repeated tiny labels and simultaneous controls make
the actual observation harder to read.

Preferred direction: **a clear observing workspace with the precision of a
scientific figure and the explanatory quality of a field notebook**. The sky
image supplies the visual character. Dates, wavelength context and evidence
form its captions. Retain a dark image well; prototype surrounding panels in
restrained charcoal and, as an alternative, a light neutral treatment. Choose
one coherent direction based on readability and real imagery. A theme switch
is not required to satisfy this brief.

| Design decision | Requirement | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Composition | One obvious main observation/comparison, one associated explanation, subordinate controls | Users should not have to decide which of twelve equally prominent boxes matters |
| Typography | Clear title/body/metadata levels; normal case for instructions; monospace for alignment-sensitive numbers | Technical depth should not require reading a screen of microtype or acronyms |
| Surfaces | Prefer spacing, aligned sections and useful separators; use cards only for genuinely distinct objects/actions | Avoid repeated rounded boxes and nested borders without information hierarchy |
| Color | Quiet neutral surfaces, one deliberate interaction accent, consistent quality colors with labels | Color should explain selection/quality rather than decorate every section |
| Effects | Avoid decorative glow, gradient headings, glass layers, ornamental grids and animated star backgrounds | Real images and meaningful annotations already carry the subject matter |
| Icons | Consistent small icon family; label important actions in words | A row of ambiguous symbols is not a simplified interface |
| Numbers | Show only decision-relevant metrics by default; units and scope stay adjacent | Decorative KPI tiles, fake precision and unexplained counts undermine understanding |
| Copy | Concrete observation/action language; remove jargon from primary navigation, retain exact terms in details | “Compare dates” helps a beginner while the method remains inspectable |
| Empty/loading states | Purposeful layout, honest status and useful action; no artificial chart or generated sky placeholder | Visual polish must not simulate evidence or progress |
| Motion | Only functional feedback and user-requested comparison; consistent brief transitions | Unnecessary motion competes with the scientific change the user is examining |
| Detail | Align dates, scales, legends, tick labels and controls; verify all states, not just the ideal screenshot | Craft is visible in consistent behavior and readable edge cases |

Do not replace this with a generic marketing landing page, a wall of feature
cards or a stylized spacecraft cockpit. The first screen should make the
observing task available. Avoid copying another product's branding. Reference
established scientific figures, observing logs and accessible image tools for
specific interaction lessons; record what each reference solves rather than
collecting an aesthetic mood board without functional criteria.

##### 5.8.2 Readability with depth: design three levels of information

Audit every current panel, metric, annotation and sentence. Assign each an
audience, a task, a scientific consequence and a default disclosure level.
Remove duplicate information and relocate optional tools; do not delete
scientific warnings or evidence to make the screenshot cleaner.

| Level | Visible content | Depth preserved |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate orientation | Field/object, actual dates, active spectral context, one comparison-status sentence, main action | Establishes what is being examined and the key limitation |
| Working explanation | Selected comparison details, usable/total measurements, uncertainty/flags, a concise “what this supports” explanation | Supports interpretation without requiring code knowledge |
| Technical inspection | Input identifiers/hashes, full flag bits, WCS, calibration/background choices, extraction parameters and replay record | Lets an expert audit the result rather than trusting simplified copy |

Use the same underlying evidence for all levels. Expanding details must not
reveal that the simple view overstated the result. The most consequential
limitation remains visible in the immediate view even if its derivation is
collapsed. Support direct access to technical details and retain selection,
scroll/focus context and scientific state when moving between levels.

Replace quantity-of-information as a proxy for sophistication with
**information available at the point it is needed**. For example, show “No
usable flux: saturated aperture” before a 240-row table; show actual compared
dates before raw epoch IDs; show the measured plane's meaning before a bare
MJy/sr readout. Advanced users should still reach the full table, identifiers
and parameters quickly. Do not duplicate the app into separate novice and
expert implementations that can diverge scientifically.

##### 5.8.3 Visual design deliverables and selection criteria

Before broad implementation, produce two low-cost layout/typography studies
using the **same real data and content**, at desktop and phone widths. Compare
the proposed calm observing-workspace direction with a deliberately simplified
version of the current dark instrument layout. Both must include a normal
comparison, a prominent limitation, an all-null spectrum and a recoverable
failure. A beautiful empty shell is not a valid design candidate.

Review each study for:

- First-screen clarity: can a new reader identify the object, dates, principal
  action and current limitation without opening Help?
- Hierarchy and readability at actual size: does the eye reach the observation
  and explanation before transfer controls and implementation metadata?
- Scientific depth: can an experienced reader locate methods, eligible samples,
  provenance and replay details without losing the comparison?
- Mobile coherence: does the same information remain understandable without
  shrinking desktop rails or hiding essential controls offscreen?
- Distinctiveness and consistency: are layout and annotations shaped by this
  project's comparison task, with typography/spacing/color used consistently?

Document why the chosen direction performs better, including disagreements
and remaining uncertainty. Preference voting alone does not establish usability.
Translate the chosen design into a small token/component specification:
type hierarchy, spacing, surfaces, semantic colors, control states, focus,
chart legends and responsive behavior. Build one complete vertical slice
(choose field → compare → inspect limitation → evidence) before applying the
style across the application. Reuse tested components, but customize their
composition and behavior to the task rather than accepting template defaults.

Add explicit review deliverables to U0/U2/U7: component/content inventory;
desktop/mobile studies; chosen-direction rationale; a real-data vertical
slice; before/after task and comprehension results; final screenshots of both
successful and unavailable results. The review is not complete if only the
hero image looks better while tables, errors or mobile sheets remain cramped.

#### 5.9 Accessibility, motion and keyboard design

Target WCAG 2.2 AA and record measured/manual outcomes rather than declaring
compliance from a component library. Follow the [W3C modal-dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/):
initial focus, forward/backward containment including focus on the shell,
inert background, Escape, and restoration to the triggering control. Avoid
nested competing dialogs. Add visible focus, correct tab order, names/states,
semantic headings and keyboard equivalents for selection/export.

Use at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and the applicable 3:1 requirements
for large text/meaningful graphical controls; measure actual tokens/states.
The [AA target-size criterion](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum)
is 24×24 CSS pixels or its defined exceptions. Use approximately 44×44 as a
practical mobile design goal, not as a misquoted AA requirement. Check adjacent
spacing, not just bounding boxes.

Reduced motion and the existing 8 FPS ceiling must remain, but **8 FPS does not
prove flash safety**. Frame changes and flashes are different measures.
[WCAG flash guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)
requires no more than three flashes per second unless below the defined
thresholds. Test actual contrast/luminance/area at the largest image size and
supported rates; retain initial pause, manual stepping and an immediate stop
control. Do not introduce full-screen transitions or brightness flashes.
If the supported blink presentation cannot satisfy thresholds, constrain its
rate/area/contrast or use step/side-by-side mode through an approved design
decision. Do not lower scientific image contrast silently to manufacture a
passing accessibility claim.

Announce completed loading, errors and selection changes through appropriate
status regions, without stealing focus or announcing every blink tick.
Provide tap-to-pin and keyboard/table equivalents for hover information.
Test screen-reader operation, 200%/400% zoom, 320px equivalent reflow, reduced
motion and touch navigation; audit canvas alternatives explicitly.
See [W3C status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html)
and [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/).

#### 5.10 Sharing, evidence export and documentation alignment

Define the reproducibility promise before adding URL parameters. A shared
analysis should preserve query/target, data revision or fingerprint, selected
observations/pair, band/composition, mode and relevant overlays. Decide whether
display transfer, camera, trial parameters and advanced-panel state belong in
the share format. If unsupported, say exactly what is restored; do not use
“exact view.” Version/validate the format and handle missing observations or
obsolete revisions with a visible notice instead of silently choosing a
different comparison. Restore paused by default, including shared links.

Evolve the triage card into a readable evidence report: A/B/difference where
eligible, actual dates/wavelength context, selected measurement counts,
approximate errors, reasons for exclusions, provenance/processing revision,
units/normalization, limitations and a reproducible link. Keep unavailable
panels explicitly unavailable. Distinguish scientific summaries from display
screenshots. Verify export figures use the same selected pair and policy as
the workspace; avoid reporting a different native spectrum as though it came
from the painted image. No new discovery score is needed.

Update Help, onboarding, labels, README screenshots and the planned user guide
from a shared vocabulary. The [documentation writing plan](plans/documentation-writing-plan.md)
defines the technical explanation behind these interactions. Write final
instructions against actual UI behavior, not old component names or sketches.

#### 5.12 Verification and acceptance

**Behavior/science cases:** saved and live requests; one/two/many observations;
incompatible pairs; missing band/reference/telemetry; partial background;
all-null spectrum (real R Draconis case); finite but unused channels; negative
flux/error ranges; saturated targets; old-cache payloads; query cancellation;
stale completion; malformed URL; outdated revision; network failure; export
failure. Controlled test responses are allowed only as inventoried isolated
tests, never product fallback data.

**Interaction matrix:** Chrome/Firefox and a Safari/WebKit check; desktop,
tablet, 390px phone, 320px reflow and small landscape; mouse, touch, keyboard,
screen reader, reduced motion and zoom. Verify modal containment in both
directions and focus restoration. Preserve important canvas functionality
without per-tick React rendering, per-tick asset requests or bitmap leaks.
Measure initial saved-data load, frame decode, playback frame times and memory
over repeated target switches on a named device; agree on budgets from the
baseline before optimization. Separate live upstream latency from local UI
responsiveness.

**Task-based usability study:** recruit at least five first-time users and
three astronomy/technical users if feasible. This is formative evidence, not
a statistically representative study. Record consented notes/timings, task
success, errors, prompts and comprehension. Use the same task definitions for
baseline and redesign; counterbalance examples/order where possible to reduce
learning effects. If participant access is unavailable, document the missing
validation and use an explicitly labeled internal walkthrough; do not call it
user-tested.

Predeclare initial design targets, then report actual results rather than
quietly changing them:

- At least 4/5 novices can open a reviewed example, identify the two dates,
  select/step a comparison and state its main limitation within two minutes,
  without facilitator instructions. Record task-specific failures, not only
  an aggregate score.
- At least 4/5 novices correctly distinguish a reference marker, a flagged
  measurement and a confirmed detection in a short comprehension check.
  The exercise must permit “none of these results is a confirmed detection”;
  it must not imply that this app has produced one.
  No interface text may label an unvalidated result a confirmed discovery.
- All three technical participants can find masks/background/wavelength
  context and export a consistent report without losing the selected pair.
- Search, band/date selection, pause/step, Spectrum and Evidence are reachable
  on mobile without horizontal scrolling to find essential actions.
- Before interaction, at least 4/5 novices can identify the field/object,
  compared dates (or that no pair is selected), main action and principal
  limitation from the initial view. Test wording and hierarchy, not recall
  after a facilitator has explained the interface.
- All technical participants can move from the concise result explanation to
  its measurement eligibility, method and lineage record without resetting
  the selected comparison. Added readability must not remove this depth.
- Visual review covers normal, partial, null, loading and failure states at
  desktop/phone sizes. No placeholder science, unexplained decorative metrics
  or pervasive tiny labels are accepted as a substitute for meaningful design.
- Every unavailable scientific result names the reason or honestly says it is
  unknown; zero usable flux cannot produce a success-looking blank chart.
- Share/restore and exports preserve the documented scientific selection and
  expose revision/availability differences explicitly.
- No unresolved critical/serious automated accessibility findings on core
  routes, plus recorded manual keyboard, screen-reader, reflow and motion
  checks. An automated scan alone is insufficient.
- Manual checks must pass modal focus containment in both directions, Escape
  and trigger-focus restoration, and completion of essential tasks at 320px
  equivalent reflow without hidden primary actions. Recording a failure is
  not sufficient to pass this gate.
- No regression in real-only serving, immutable raw measurements, scientific
  masks, normalization, API semantics or retained science failures.

**Exit gate:** a newcomer can complete and explain a real supported comparison;
an advanced user can inspect and reproduce its evidence; failures remain
understandable and recoverable; mobile and keyboard users can access the core
workflow. This UI gate does not override unresolved Phase 4 science gates.

### Phase 6 — Release verification and submission (formerly Phase 5)

1. Run backend regression tests, frontend typecheck/build, saved-data integrity, raw science checks, and the real-only package inventory.
2. Exercise actual browser search, click coordinates, epoch stepping, difference controls, stale-request cancellation, URL restore, missing bands, and clean failures. Confirm reduced-motion behavior and the 8 FPS cap. This audit did not run that browser matrix.
3. Build and inspect the final container. Repeat catalog/offline and cold-live tests through its real HTTP/static-serving path, including requests for removed fixtures.
4. Verify the public URL in an unauthenticated browser, proxy timeouts, persistent cache, bounded disk usage, and expected small-demo concurrency. Confirm repository/judge access and current event requirements from official sources.
5. Update README/STATUS/demo claims from measured results. Distinguish real origin, usable photometry, visualization quality, and validated astrophysical interpretation.

Release gate: no shipped synthetic observations or model spectra; arbitrary supported coordinates work geometrically; available compatible multi-epoch data can actually blink; outages remain honest; scientific claims pass independent checks; public access is verified. Until then, describe the project as a working real-data prototype with the limitations above.

## Execution order and scope control

Do Phase 1 and the TAN correction first. Then implement discovery/deadline changes and the wavelength/mask validation together: increasing epoch count alone would expose more scientifically ambiguous differences. Resolve the scientific failures and rebuild into a separately validated Phase 4 cache. Execute the Phase 5 UI overhaul against that reviewed data and meaning. Finish with Phase 6 browser/container/public-access verification and submission. Documentation drafting begins with the evidence ledger and stable technical chapters now; final worked examples, user instructions and screenshots follow Phases 4–5.

Do not spend effort on unrelated visualization features, full 102-channel cube integration, PSF-wing recovery, or discovery claims ahead of these gates. The authorized UI plan improves the core comparison, evidence and navigation workflow; it is not an invitation to expand every experimental tool. This artifact records plans and historical audit evidence. Its 2026-11-14 planning update changes no application code, saved observations or API contract.
